Dynasties & Eras of India

c. 319–550 CE 🏛️ Pataliputra

Gupta Empire (Classical Golden Age)

The Golden Age of ancient India marked by monumental advancements in mathematics (the concept of zero and decimal system), astronomy, Sanskrit literature, and Hindu-Buddhist art.

848–1279 CE 🏛️ Thanjavur / Gangaikonda Cholapuram

Imperial Chola Dynasty

Dominant maritime empire of South India renowned for bronze sculpting, grand Dravidian stone temples (Brihadisvara), and naval expeditions across Southeast Asia.

322–185 BCE 🏛️ Pataliputra (Patna)

Maurya Empire

First pan-Indian empire founded by Chandragupta Maurya and philosopher Chanakya, reaching its moral and philosophical pinnacle under Emperor Ashoka the Great.

1526–1857 CE 🏛️ Agra / Delhi / Fatehpur Sikri

Mughal Empire

Cosmopolitan Indo-Persian Islamic empire renowned for administrative unification, religious synthesis under Akbar, and peerless architectural masterworks like the Taj Mahal.

Vijayanagara Empire

Rulers & Emperors (7)

268–232 BCE

Ashoka the Great

Ashoka was an Indian emperor of the Maurya Dynasty, who ruled almost all of the Indian subcontinent from c. 268 to 232 BCE. He promoted the spread of Buddhism across ancient Asia.

Biography →
322–298 BCE

Chandragupta Maurya

Founder of the Maurya Empire with guidance from his guru Chanakya (Kautilya). Defeated Seleucus I Nicator and unified the Indian subcontinent from Bengal to Afghanistan under centralized rule.

Biography →
268–232 BCE

Emperor Ashoka the Great (Devanampriya Priyadasi)

Renowned pan-Indian emperor who, remorseful after the conquest of Kalinga, converted to Buddhism and dedicated his reign to Dhamma (righteousness), erecting the Pillars of Ashoka and sending Buddhist peace missions worldwide.

Biography →
1556–1605 CE

Jalal-ud-din Muhammad Akbar the Great

Third Mughal emperor who tripled the empire's size through military strategy, abolished the sectarian jizya tax, patronized interfaith dialogues at the Ibadat Khana, and created the syncretic Din-i Ilahi.

Biography →
985–1014 CE

Rajaraja Chola I the Great

Visionary king who transformed the Chola kingdom into an extensive maritime empire. Conducted land surveys, built a formidable navy, and constructed the colossal granite Brihadisvara Temple at Thanjavur.

Biography →
c. 335–375 CE

Samudragupta (Napoleon of India)

Great military ruler and patron of the arts recorded on the Allahabad Pillar. Conquered numerous kingdoms across the subcontinent while mastering poetry, music (playing the veena), and Vedic scholarship.

Biography →
1628–1658 CE

Shah Jahan (King of the World)

The master builder of the Mughal Empire who commissioned the Taj Mahal in Agra as a mausoleum for his wife Mumtaz Mahal, as well as the Red Fort and Jama Masjid in Delhi.

Biography →
